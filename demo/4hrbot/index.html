4 · Entry Intelligence

The saved daily FRED report, crypto and asset context, relevant themes, verified news and available upcoming events check whether the setup faces material opposing risk.

Macro, context, news and event rules
  • Macro: score ≤25 blocks longs; score ≥75 blocks shorts. Between these limits there is no macro-direction block. The score is neither a trading allocation nor a success probability.
  • The saved 07:00 Brisbane reporting cycle must match: before 07:00 use the previous day, then today's report. Macro coverage must be at least 50%.
  • Market context: snapshot age ≤11 minutes; crypto and asset layers must remain valid, fresh and at least 60% covered. Bias ≤−50 blocks longs; bias ≥50 blocks shorts. Fresh relevant themes use the same directional threshold; unavailable themes are recorded as gaps.
  • News: at least one enabled provider must have succeeded within two hours. Relevant, uncontradicted primary-source events with confidence ≥90% can block for critical veto-capable incidents or strong adverse high-impact evidence. General headlines and code releases do not become directional predictions.
  • Upcoming events: require at least one healthy saved primary calendar, updated within 30 hours. Known high-impact events block applicable entries from 60 minutes before until 30 minutes after. Date-only events cover the full source date; date ranges cover the full range. Macro events apply to all pairs.
  • BEA releases and Federal Reserve meetings are connected when healthy. Coverage is partial: BLS availability depends on source health; token unlocks, ETF deadlines and governance feeds are not connected, and other global events can be missing. Individual gaps are recorded; they are not treated as a complete calendar. Testnet dates are not assumed to be mainnet catalysts.
  • Missing, stale, invalid or insufficient required data, guard transport failure, or a decision-recording failure blocks new entries.

Exit monitoring

Once a position opens, the existing strategy manages exits independently of the entry guard. A trade can close through a candle exit signal, its stop-loss, a profit target or its trailing stop. Events can block new positions while an existing trade remains open.

Exact exit, profit-target and trailing rules
  • Candle exit signal: evaluated using the last closed four-hour candle, with non-zero volume. Long: close < EMA50 with RSI <48, or MACD < its signal line with RSI <50. Short: close > EMA50 with RSI >52, or MACD > its signal line with RSI >50. An exit signal can close a losing trade.
  • Minimum profit target: the bot can request an exit once its calculated trade profit exceeds the target for the trade's age: 6% before 6 hours, 3.5% from 6 to 12 hours, 2% from 12 to 24 hours, then 1% from 24 hours onward. These are elapsed hours from trade opening. Reaching an age boundary lowers the target; it does not automatically close the trade. For example, a trade over 24 hours old with 0.5% profit has not reached its 1% target.
  • Stop-loss: configured at −6%. If the monitored price reaches the active stop, the bot requests a market exit. Stops are monitored by the bot; this configuration does not place a standing stop order on the exchange. Execution price and costs can make the final loss differ from 6%.
  • Trailing stop: after profit moves above the 3.5% offset, the stop follows the best price at a 2% distance. It tightens as price moves in the trade's favour and does not move back to allow a larger loss. A reversal to that stop can request an exit before the next candle closes. The profit target may close the trade before trailing protection is used.
  • How a close completes: stops and profit targets are checked during the bot's processing loop, separately from four-hour candle signals. Regular signal and profit-target exits use limit orders; stop-loss exits use market orders. A close is complete only when the exit order fills in this paper simulation. A signal or target being reached is not proof of a completed close.
